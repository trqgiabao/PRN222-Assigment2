AI-Assisted Semantic-Aware Framework for Microservice Boundary Identification Using Heterogeneous Software Knowledge Graphs, Graph Neural Networks, and Large Language Models
1. Research Questions
Background
The migration from monolithic architectures to microservices has become a critical modernization strategy for enterprise software systems. Microservices provide scalability, fault isolation, independent deployment, and technological flexibility. However, identifying appropriate service boundaries remains one of the most challenging stages of migration.
Most existing decomposition approaches rely primarily on structural dependencies extracted from source code. While these approaches can identify technical relationships, they often fail to capture business semantics, domain boundaries, and architectural intent. Consequently, generated microservices may exhibit poor business alignment and evolve into distributed monoliths.
Recent advances in Artificial Intelligence provide new opportunities for addressing these limitations. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) can learn structural patterns from software dependency graphs, while Large Language Models (LLMs) can understand semantic information embedded in source code, documentation, comments, and naming conventions.
Therefore, this research proposes an AI-assisted semantic-aware framework that integrates structural intelligence and semantic intelligence to improve microservice boundary identification.

2.1 Monolithic to Microservice Migration
Microservice architecture decomposes software systems into independently deployable and loosely coupled services. This architectural style improves scalability, maintainability, and deployment flexibility.
However, migration from monolithic systems remains difficult because legacy applications often contain tightly coupled modules, shared databases, and undocumented business rules.

N?u vi?t trong Literature Review
B?n có th? thêm do?n này:
The proposed framework is built upon three major research streams. First, it extends the findings of Hassan et al. (2024), which identified service decomposition as the most critical challenge in monolithic-to-microservice migration. Second, it leverages advances in Graph Neural Networks for software structure analysis, where software systems are modeled as graphs of interconnected entities and relationships. Third, it incorporates Large Language Models to capture business semantics embedded in source code, documentation, and naming conventions. By integrating these research directions into a unified framework, this study addresses the limitations of existing structural-only decomposition approaches and provides a more comprehensive solution for microservice boundary identification.
